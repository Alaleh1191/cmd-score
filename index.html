CMD Score

Clinical prediction of coronary microvascular dysfunction (PET myocardial flow reserve < 2) · derived and externally validated in the REFINE PET registry

Intended use. Patients with angina or angina-equivalent symptoms in whom obstructive epicardial coronary artery disease has been reasonably excluded by first-line testing (suspected ANOCA), to estimate the likelihood of reduced myocardial flow reserve and support decisions about referral for quantitative assessment of coronary vasodilator function. Not validated in patients with prior CABG, heart transplantation, or abnormal perfusion. For research and educational use; it does not replace clinical judgement or diagnostic testing.

Patient values

BSA by the Mosteller formula: √(height × weight / 3600).

Model equation and important caveats

Predicted probability = 1 / (1 + e−L), where

L = −4.4846 + 0.018551 × age − 0.011766 × eGFR + 0.025495 × heart rate + 0.004234 × SBP + 0.008441 × LV mass index + 0.17731 × diabetes + 0.42108 × PVD

Diabetes and PVD are coded 1 if present and 0 if absent. Coefficients come from a LASSO logistic regression (λ1SE) derived in 3,257 patients at one centre and externally validated in 6,630 patients at eight centres (AUROC 0.76, 95% CI 0.75–0.77).

  • Population. Derived and validated in patients referred for clinical rest–stress PET with normal relative perfusion (summed stress score ≤ 3) and without prior CABG or heart transplantation. Performance in other settings, including general cardiology populations before advanced imaging, has not been established.
  • Outcome. CMD was defined as PET global myocardial flow reserve < 2. Reduced MFR is a surrogate of CMD; it does not capture endothelium-dependent dysfunction or microvascular spasm and may also reflect diffuse epicardial disease or high resting flow.
  • LV mass index. Measured from PET in the study cohorts. Echocardiographic or CMR values are not interchangeable and have not been validated with this model.
  • Calibration. In external validation the model overestimated risk on average (calibration-in-the-large −0.36) and its probabilities were compressed (calibration slope 1.70). Interpret the absolute probability with caution; the observed prevalence in the matching decile is shown for reference. Local recalibration may be needed before clinical use.
  • Threshold. The 23% threshold was chosen in the derivation cohort to give sensitivity and NPV of at least 80% while maximizing specificity. It is intended to identify patients in whom reduced MFR is less likely, not to diagnose CMD.

Values outside the range seen in the external validation cohort (1st–99th percentile) are flagged; predictions for such values are extrapolations.

Reference: Azhir A, et al. A Clinical Prediction Tool to Identify Coronary Microvascular Dysfunction. [Journal, year; DOI to be added on publication.] · For research and educational use only; not a medical device and not a substitute for clinical judgement.
